## 1. What we are building

**Companion** (working name) is a desktop app, Mac first and Windows next on one codebase, for people who start projects with AI and get stuck. Abstract characters with their own personalities live on the side edges of the screen. Through them the user gets one companion that helps them finish what they start. The promise: **you finish what you start**. Projects get built, checked and shipped with proof, and the user never sits stuck guessing what to ask or where to click.

- **Who it is for:** the **Curious path**: non engineers (students, non technical founders, creators). The **Geeked (Pro) path** for engineers is parked: build nothing for it, block nothing for it.
- **Bring your own Claude.** Users bring their own Claude: their own API key (stored in the macOS Keychain), their own cloud credential, or, only once Anthropic confirms in writing, their Claude subscription signed in through Anthropic's own flow. We never pay for, resell or route anyone's model usage. We charge for the companion layer (a subscription with Free and Plus tiers), never a model markup.
- **Distribution:** direct download from our own website, not an app store.
- **It is a productivity tool, never an "AI friend".** Plain language in everything a user sees. No affection, streaks or mood check ins.

### The shared core

Every feature is a thin client of four parts: **characters**, **agent engine**, **memory**, **trust layer** (snapshots, proof, receipts, approvals, safety). `docs/architecture.md` maps every feature to the modules that own it, so later features only ever mean new modules plugged into existing seams.

### The characters (placeholder names, all renameable, switchable skins)

| Character | Shape | Personality | Default agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spark | small flame or star | fast, tries things quickly | Builder |
| Prism | faceted triangle | careful, checks before moving | Reviewer |
| Loop | ring | persistent, keeps fixing | Rescuer |
| Orb | soft blob | calm, explains | Tutor, and Guide (Show me how) |
| Pebble | smooth stone | steady, says little | Shipper |

The Researcher has no shape yet. Closer, Watcher, Analyst and Supervisor get shapes when their modes arrive. Agents are jobs; characters are identities; any character can run any agent. The characters dock on the **side edges** of the screen (never the Mac notch), can be moved anywhere, show a clear on and working indicator, and expand into cards with proof, plus Stop, Undo and Receipt. Motion is the signature asset: Spark quick and bouncy, Prism precise, Loop rolling, Orb drifting, Pebble grounded.

## 2. The features: presence plus four jobs

**Presence** (the companion is always there)
- **Characters and dock:** edge docked, with states, cards with proof, presentation mode (it checks only whether a call app is in front and whether the camera or microphone is in use, never content; can be turned off).
- **Ask box:** a global hotkey opens a small input beside the dock. Type or hold to talk. Drag a folder onto a character to start. The right agent starts.
- **Voice:** hold a hotkey and speak; transcribed on the device; replies are text, read aloud only if turned on.
- **Stop everything:** one global hotkey stops every agent, task, capture and the microphone.
- **Onboarding questionnaire:** optional, at most five questions, each answer visibly changes behaviour.
- **Remote approvals (Beta):** a phone web app and Telegram, through our own relay. Not WhatsApp (Meta bars general purpose assistants).

**Job 1: help you use AI better**
- **Prompt help:** a hotkey in any app. It reads the prompt being typed, rewrites it using project context, shows a diff, and pastes it back on accept.
- **Show me how (the "Clicky" feature):** a hotkey takes one capture of the screen and a pointer shows where to click, step by step.

**Job 2: do the work (Build mode)**
- **Drive:** the Builder plans short steps, runs each through Claude Code, checkpoints, and proves each one. "Take the wheel" (opening the project's editor or terminal, or editing a file yourself) pauses it.
- **Decision cards:** at a real fork, two or three options with a recommendation, saved so it is never asked twice.
- **Rescue:** reads a stuck project, names the cause in two sentences, gets it running, writes a finish plan.
- **Ship:** safety gate, preview deploy, live on one click, rollback.
- **Research:** looks up what the build needs and files cited notes into project memory; writes no code.
- **Proof and review:** the proof ladder on every "done" (exit codes, tests, a browser check, screenshot evidence, then a second model reviews).
- **Undo, receipts, timeline:** a snapshot before every step, one click undo, a receipt per task.
- **House rules:** corrections like "never touch the auth folder" become rules every agent follows.
- **Fix loop breaker:** after three failed fixes on one error, it stops and offers another approach, an undo, or a question.
- **Beta:** project shelf, up to three parallel projects, a 30 second build timelapse built from proof screenshots (never a screen recording), launch post, removable "built with" link.

**Job 3: notice for you**
- **Where was I (Alpha):** on return, three lines: what you were doing, what finished, what waits on you.
- **v1.1:** recaps; **Loop Closer** (catches promises and stalled projects in sources the user connects; drafts the follow up; never sends without a tap); **Pattern Catcher** (notices work you repeat and offers to automate it after one approval).

**Job 4: grow with you**
- **Explain (Alpha):** after each task, two or three plain "why" bullets and one optional check question; on by default for Curious. This is the tutoring that ships first.
- **Memory (Alpha):** project memory plus a "how I work with you" profile, shown in an editable "What I know about you" page.
- **Tutor (Later):** a "do it with me" mode that pauses at one real decision per task and teaches, with quizzes and visible progress. Part of the later **Learn** mode (also a classroom plan).

**Later modes:** Learn, Work (analyst, meeting notes to actions), Team (oversight of many agents and people), more engines (Cursor, Codex, a second model vendor), project memory exposed as an MCP server, cloud agents. Each mode is gated on retention (F08).

### The new launch priority (founder, 8 Oct 2026, record F15)

Companion is for people coding with AI. Highest priority first:
1. **Do the work (agents)**: Build mode is "the best part", with the best possible Claude prompting underneath. Agents first (F03).
2. **Prompt help while you code.**
3. **Shared memory.**
4. **Show me how last.** Still in the launch scope (F07), built after the other three.

Other points: logic first, looks later. A beautiful, calm, professional **website** to download from, and a calm, professional **onboarding flow**, both later; the website should help the company grow beyond the product (content, changelog, community; scope open). **Prompt help should feel like this:** the user starts Companion while coding, it can ask clarifying questions, and gives recommendations either as a prompt to copy or written straight into the box (paste back with a diff). The user can press a hotkey or talk, and it answers by typing or by voice, in a clean, simple, changeable style, **not chat bubbles**.

**Parked Pro plan idea:** for people who already run agents, a **swarm of connected memory**: one shared memory all their agents read and write, so any one can do the work with everything the others learned. Nothing is built for it now; memory stays plain files in a vendor neutral shape so it can plug in later. The thinking: many people already have agents, so Companion should make their lives easier than "make an agent and let it work", through memory, proof and prompt quality.

**Two points, both confirmed by the founder on 8 Oct 2026 (F15):**
1. **Hotkey only.** The founder's picture said the companion would "see everything" while the user codes. The rules forbid watching in the background (rules 5, 6, 11), so Prompt help works only when the user presses the hotkey (or holds to talk): Companion reads that one prompt with a visible indicator, then asks "Want recommendations?". It never speaks up unasked after typing in another app.
2. **Clarifying questions.** "It will ask me a lot of questions" means short clarifying questions inside Prompt help, not a longer first run questionnaire (which stays capped at five, F05).

## 5. The rules that never bend (AGENTS.md)

**Claude usage.** (1) Never pay for, resell or route anyone's Claude usage. (2) Never show a Claude login form; never read, store or pass claude.ai tokens or credential files. Plan login happens only in Anthropic's own `claude auth login`. `claude auth status` runs only while signing in or on "Check again", never at launch or on a timer. The API key stays in the Keychain and reaches only the runner and Claude Code; agent commands, hooks and MCP servers never see it. (3) The API key route is the polished default; the subscription route sits behind a remote switch, off until Anthropic confirms in writing. (4) Ship Anthropic's Claude binary unmodified and never re-sign it; never call or style anything as "Claude Code" ("Powered by Claude" is fine).

**Never watch passively.** (5) The user's screen, other apps' text boxes and the microphone are touched only on a user hotkey, with a visible indicator lit first; never in the background, never stored; captures of the user's screen are never written to disk (proof screenshots of the app being built, in a headless browser, are stored with receipts). (6) The one exception: a source the user explicitly connected for Loop Closer or Pattern Catcher, read on their schedule, indexed on the device, deleted on disconnect. (7) Presentation mode checks only whether a call app is in front and whether the camera or microphone is in use. (8) Each permission is asked the first time its feature is used, never at install; nothing asks for Input Monitoring.

**Hands off the user's things.** (9) Never steal keyboard focus; panels are non activating. (10) Never write the user's git (see F13). (11) One global hotkey stops everything.

**Safety and proof.** (12) Safety lives in hooks, deny rules and allowlists, never in prompts. (13) Never send, pay, delete or go live without a tap; unattended tasks never exceed their allowlist; never use `bypassPermissions`. (14) "Done" means proven: exit codes first, browser checks second, a screenshot judgment only as extra evidence.

**Memory and tone.** (15) Memory is always visible, with its source, forgettable; never health, money, relationships, moods or secrets. (16) A productivity tool, never an AI friend.

**How we build.** (17) Tests first for risky logic (snapshots, undo, safety, paste back, redaction, permissions, memory scope). (18) Items marked **[verify]** are checked on a real Mac before code depends on them. (19) Founder decisions are not reopened; technical records change only when a spike proves the research wrong, and the founder is told. (20) Pin the Agent SDK to an exact version and always run its bundled binary. (21) Names live in one strings file.

## 8. Spike (a): what we proved about the screen edge panel

**The question.** Can a Tauri window sit on the screen edge as a non activating panel, show over full screen apps and on every Space, and never take the keyboard from the app the user is typing in, on macOS 27?

**Environment.** macOS 27.0.1, MacBook Pro, Apple Silicon, built in display only. Rust 1.99, Tauri 2.12.1, wry 0.57, tao 0.37.1.

**What failed.**
- The research's recipe (tauri-nspanel: turn a window into a panel, let it become key only while typing) fails on macOS 27. A click on the panel makes the whole app active and takes the keyboard (known open bug: tauri-nspanel issue 123, no fix posted). Reproduced 4 of 4 times, and again in the founder's live session.
- The planned plan B (a docked panel that never takes the keyboard, with typing in a separate card) also failed: a click on it still brought the app to the front, and so did a click inside the card. Only the keyboard route (hotkey, type, Esc) worked.

**What worked.**
- Spike a2: a panel written in plain AppKit, created non activating from the start, kept the user's typing through 13 clicks with no web view, and 25 clicks with a web view inside.
- Spike a3: Tauri's own web pages inside such a panel, for the character and the typing card: 25 clicks on the character, the hotkey, type and Esc route, and two clicks inside the card (logged both by the page and by a native mouse monitor) all left the front app unchanged.

**The cause.** Not the web view and not macOS 27 itself: tauri-nspanel creates an ordinary window first and adds the "don't activate" flag afterwards, which on macOS 27 does not fully take effect. A panel created with the flag from the start works.

**The decision (T01, founder approved).** Create each panel ourselves with `NSPanel initWithContentRect:styleMask:backing:defer:` and the non activating flag in that call. Character: borderless (never takes the keyboard). Card: titled, with a hidden title bar (can take the keyboard for typing). Build the Tauri window hidden, move its web view into the panel, show with `orderFrontRegardless`, hide with `orderOut`, position with `setFrame`.

**Research (public sources).** The tauri-nspanel issue is open with no replies; wry asks macOS to activate the app once when a page is built (not on click); macOS 26 tightened app activation rules; the open source app Clicky avoids the problem by making its overlays ignore clicks entirely, so it offers no technique for a clickable character; no public reports from similar apps about macOS 27 clicks.

**Other things learned.** Wispr Flow keeps an invisible, click through overlay at the highest window level over the right 490 points of the screen. The founder's iCloud synced Desktop folder adds file tags that break code signing, so some builds are done outside it. Computer use cannot drive this test, so the clicks have to be the founder's.

**Not yet checked (moved to M2, re run on the new construction).** Opening from Finder; full screen apps; Spaces and Stage Manager; Dock position and screen size changes; a second display; the System Settings privacy lists; design variants with first click off. Two specific M2 re-checks: (1) while the card holds the keyboard, the app privately reports itself as active although the front app never changes, so check for side effects; (2) clicking the card while it is visible but not key, after the user switched to another app (in spike (a) that click activated the app; a3 only tested clicks while the card already had the keyboard).

## 12. How to work with the founder

Plain words, short sentences, no em dashes, compound words written open (on device, non activating, hold to talk). For hands on tests: guide one step at a time in chat, one action, at most one thing to paste, one yes or no question, and wait for the answer; do the setup and record the answers yourself. Never ask for keys in chat. Show docs changes before committing; commit only when asked.

## 13. Words used

- **Spike:** a small throwaway experiment that answers one yes or no technical question.
- **Panel (NSPanel):** a floating window that can stay out of the way of the app the user is typing in.
- **Non activating:** clicking it does not make its app the front app or take the keyboard.
- **Sidecar:** a helper program shipped inside the app, started by it. Our runner is one.
- **Notarization:** Apple's automatic malware check. **Ad hoc signing:** a temporary signature made on one's own Mac, fine for testing.
- **Proof ladder:** the ordered checks behind every "done" (exit codes, tests, browser check, screenshot, second model review).
- **Shadow snapshots:** our own saved copies of the project before each step, kept outside the user's git, used by Undo.
- **House rules:** user corrections that every agent follows.
