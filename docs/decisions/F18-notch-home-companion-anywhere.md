# F18: Home in the notch, the companion comes from anywhere, two key hold to talk (17 Oct 2026)

- **Status:** Founder decision, 17 Oct 2026. Amends F04 (docking) and F17 (launch scope). Not to be reopened without the founder.
- **Source:** the founder, in the build session of 17 Oct 2026: "the docking system and everything about the settings will stay in the notch. The companion can come in from any part of the Mac, wherever you keep it, wherever it has something." "I would prefer a two-click hold to talk." General assistant features: "for now, let's not do it. You can build it out, but I don't want to launch it with that."

## Decision
1. **Home lives in the notch.** Companion's home base (status, the card, Settings, everything you manage) sits at the top center of the screen, around the Mac's notch. It is quiet when nothing is happening and opens downward when you hover or click. On a display without a notch (an external monitor, an older Mac, and Windows later) the same home sits at the top center as a small pill. This **reverses F04's "never the Mac notch"**; F04's reason (it must work on Windows and external monitors) is met by the pill.
2. **The companion comes in from anywhere.** The character is no longer limited to the left and right edges. It lives on whatever edge or corner the person drags it to, per display, and when it has something to show (a problem, a step, a result) it comes to that place, next to the window it is about, never over it, and goes back afterwards. The motion is themed later with the character art (the founder's water idea: it surfaces from the edge). Reduced motion: it appears in place, no flight.
3. **Hold to talk is two keys.** The default is a two key chord, held while speaking and let go to stop. A modifier alone, a double tap or the Fn key are not possible (they need key events, rule 8). The default is Option and Space. If another app already owns it, Companion says so in plain words and the person picks another two key chord in Settings (a short list of chords that are free on this Mac). Control and Space is not offered by default because macOS uses it to switch keyboard languages.
4. **General assistant features are not in the launch.** HeyClicky style agents with memory, app connections, routines, dictation into any app and clicking for you stay out of the friends preview and the launch. They may be built later behind a switch that is off, only when the founder asks.
5. **Part B (rebindable hotkeys, voice settings, accessibility pass, small gaps)** is decided later, when it is needed.

## What stays
Every AGENTS.md rule. In particular: the home and the character are non activating panels and never take keyboard focus by themselves (rule 9); the screen is touched only on a hotkey with the light first (rule 5); nothing reads key events (rule 8); presentation mode and hiding from screen sharing apply to the home and the character (rule 7, M11).

## Settled in the build (item 1, 17 Oct 2026)
Details the build settled, so the hands on session has something concrete to judge. The founder can change any of them; none changes a decision above.
- **The island.** A fourth native panel (`app/src-tauri/src/island.rs`, page `app/island.html`), borderless and non activating, so it can never take the keyboard. On a notch it is exactly the notch (the notch is read from macOS: the safe area at the top, and the menu bar either side of the camera). Without a notch it is a 120 point black pill at the top center, inside the menu bar. Where no menu bar shows (it hides itself, or a second display has none) it is a thin line on the top edge, so it never sits on the front app's own window. It never grows sideways: the part of it inside the menu bar is always the notch or the pill, so the app menus and status icons beside the notch stay clickable. It only grows downward, below the menu bar. When a question waits, while listening, or just after something is done (never just because work is going on), a small lip with a mark shows under the notch; the pill shows the mark in itself, and the thin line grows into the pill. When the pointer rests on it for 0.4 seconds (crossing the notch along the menu bar does not open it), it opens downward with the status (ready, working on what, needs you for what, done with what), on up to three lines at the notch's or the pill's width.
- **Home.** The card itself, moved: it drops down from the island, centered, inside the work area, 380 points wide (room for its tabs) and up to 640 tall. A click on the island, a click on the character, or the card hotkey opens it. It has five tabs: Now, What I did, Learn, Memory, Settings. The tab that needs the person (a question, the loop card, a lesson step, the practice) shows by itself, once per new need, except while the person is typing on another tab (then that tab is marked and announced). Notices and errors about Companion itself (the engine stopped, an error from something done on any tab) show above the tabs, on every tab. During a lesson, what Show me says (Screen Recording is needed, it could not find that, the window changed, the click missed) shows under the lesson on Learn, and a new one brings Learn forward. Esc closes Home as before; a click in another app closes it too, unless something on it is waiting for the person (a question, a lesson, a pointer on the screen, Prompt help, the first run).
- **Which display.** The island lives on the display the person uses. When Home opens, it moves to the display with the pointer. Otherwise it follows the person: once the pointer has stayed on another display for 3 seconds, it moves there (a quick trip does not move it, and it stays put while Home is open). It remembers the last one (`island.json`), else the pointer's, else the primary one.
- **What it may notice.** The pointer as entering and leaving the island's own window. With more than one display, twice a second, which display the pointer is on (its position only, read without any permission). While Home is open, whether the mouse button is down and where the pointer is, to notice a click elsewhere. Never keys, never what is on the screen.
- **With item 2.** Home has one place, used both to put it there and for the character to keep clear of it (`island::card_spot`): under the island, or beside the character's home only when no display is known for the island. The character's own place on any edge or corner keeps clear of the island at its biggest and of where Home reaches at its widest and tallest (380 by 640 points, with an 8 point gap), on every display, since the island follows the person from display to display. So Home never covers the character and the character never has to move out of its way when Home opens. On a laptop this only moves a character on the top edge to the side of that column (and one on the bottom edge on a small display, where Home reaches that far down). A trip beside a window keeps clear of the island's zone always and of Home only while Home is open, at its tallest, so a closed Home costs a trip no room.

## Settled in the build (item 2, 17 Oct 2026)
These are build details, not new founder decisions; the founder can change any of them.
- **Where it lives.** Any of the four edges with a position along it; the two ends of each edge are the corners, and dragging near a corner (within 140 points of both edges) puts it exactly in the corner. Saved per display in `dock.json` as before. A place saved before F18 (left or right with a height) reads back unchanged, so nobody's character moves. It stays inside the work area and out of a clear zone at the top centre of every display (360 by 120 points below the menu bar: a notch of about 190 points plus a margin each side, and never less than the island of item 1 at its biggest), and out of Home's drop down area below it (item 1, With item 2); on the top edge it slides to the nearer side of that column.
- **When it comes.** When a ring is drawn in a confirmed window (Point at the problem, a step of a Show me answer or a lesson, a your turn ring) and when a result is about another app's window (the error explainer, Prompt help). Not for a ring with no confirmed window under it (a menu, a dialog, the desktop): it stays home.
- **Where it goes.** Beside the window, outside it, on the side with the most room, level with the target, never over the target, the window, the clear zone or Companion's card, and only on the display the target is on. When there is no room outside the window (a window that fills the screen) it stays home, and it stays home when home is already about as near.
- **The card.** Since item 1 the card is Home, under the island. While it is open the trip keeps clear of it where it is, at the tallest it can grow (640 points), not only its height when the trip starts, so the card growing as a result comes in never ends over it. If the card opens after the trip was planned, or could now reach the character, the visit is planned again from the same window: one move to a clear spot, or home when there is none.
- **The arrow.** The arrow leaves from where the character is when the ring is drawn (home, or wherever it is on its way), and the character follows it to the window. The arrow is not held back for the trip, so the ring, its words and the watch of its window stay in step. A later step of the same answer flies from the last target, as before.
- **When it goes back.** When neither a ring nor the card has been up for a second and a half; at once when the window moves, closes or is covered, another app comes to the front, the setting is turned off or Stop everything is pressed; with no trip when Companion is hidden. A drag or a display change gives it a new home and ends the visit.
- **Motion.** A short arc, 0.3 to 0.7 seconds, with the arrow's easing and bend. It never moves while the person drags it or while the pointer is over it (it holds still and goes on when the pointer leaves). Named states for the art: surface, travel, settle, visiting, return, home, each with the way to face. With reduced motion it appears in place.
- **Setting.** "Come to what it is about", on unless turned off. Home stays under the island; it goes beside the character's home only when no display is known for the island.
- **Screen sharing.** "Hide from screen sharing" now covers the character too, as this record says.

## Settled in the build (17 Oct 2026, item 3)
Details inside item 3, not changes to it.
- Option and Space is registered through the same global shortcut plugin as every other hotkey: the Mac's hot key service reports the keys going down and coming up. Nothing reads key events and nothing asks for Input Monitoring.
- The short list in Settings is Option and Space, Option and Return, and Control and Return. No two key chord is unused on a Mac, so each gives something up while it is hold to talk: Option and Space types a non breaking space on many keyboards, and the two others are macOS's own text keys (Option and Return adds a new line that does not send, in Messages and many other apps; Control and Return adds a line break). Settings says this beside each of the two. Left out on purpose, because losing them would hurt more: Control and Space (keyboard languages), Command and Space (Spotlight), Command and Backquote (the next window of an app), Control and Backquote (the terminal in Cursor and VS Code), Option and Backquote (types accents such as à on a US keyboard, and the key has another label on other keyboards), anything with Tab, Option and Escape (Speak selection), Command and Return (sends in many apps), Shift with anything (pressed by accident while typing), and letters, digits or symbols. (Corrected 17 Oct 2026: the first version of this line said the list leaves out every chord macOS uses, which was not true of the two Return chords.)
- Settings offers only the chords that are free on this Mac: when it opens, it registers each chord not in use and lets it go again at once, and a chord macOS refuses cannot be pressed, with one line saying another app uses it. Pressing a chord tries it again; if another app took it in the meantime, nothing changes and Settings says so in one line.
- That check, and the one at launch, are only as good as macOS refusing a chord another app holds. macOS refuses only when that app asked for the keys alone; Alfred, Raycast and ChatGPT use Option and Space by default and most likely do not, so Companion and that app may share the keys with no notice. Until a real Mac settles it (T13, a launch blocker), Settings, the first run and INSTALL.md name those apps and say what to do if one of them stops opening or talking does nothing.
- The choice is kept on this Mac (`talk-keys.json` in Companion's folder; delete everything removes it; since wave 4 it is kept with every other key in `keys.json`, see below). At launch the saved chord is tried, then Option and Space, and nothing else is taken without the person asking. One notice at launch says which keys another app holds and points to Settings, Keys to remember.
- Key repeats while the keys are held restart nothing. A let go that never arrives cannot leave hold to talk stuck, and the microphone still closes after a minute.
- The old Control, Option, Command and V is dropped, not kept as a second binding: nobody has used hold to talk yet (F17), and a hotkey that no hint names would take a key from other apps for nothing.

## Settled in the build (wave 4 item 1, 17 Oct 2026): every key can be changed
Item 5 above left rebindable hotkeys for later; the wave 4 plan (`docs/milestones/M11-wave4-plan.md`) took it up. These are build details, not new founder decisions; the founder can change any of them.
- **One key map for every job** (`app/src-tauri/src/keymap.rs`): open the ask box, Point at the problem, Show me how, Prompt help (read, and use a suggestion while a result shows), the error explainer, hold to talk and Stop everything. Each is a chord, one or more modifiers with one key, through the same global shortcut plugin: nothing reads key events, nothing asks for Input Monitoring, never a modifier alone, a double tap or Fn. The bare Esc that dismisses a Prompt help result is not on any list.
- **The usual keys stay exactly as they were**: Control, Option and Command with K, F, S, R, A, E and X; Option and Space for hold to talk.
- **A short list for each job**, not a key recorder (a recorder would read key presses): a letter job can take its own letter with Shift added, or one of six spare letters (I, J, L, O, P, U, chosen because they sit in the same place on QWERTY, QWERTZ and AZERTY keyboards) with Control, Option and Command. Hold to talk keeps its own two key list from item 3. No job's usual keys are on another job's list, so falling back to them never takes another job's keys.
- **Rules**: no two jobs share keys; keys another app holds show as taken (the same free check as item 3, run for one job when Change is pressed); keys another job has show as used by Companion. A change registers the new keys before the old ones are let go and is saved only if it works, so nothing is ever left without keys by a change, and Stop everything never loses working keys. Reset to the usual keys does the same for every job, and a job whose usual keys another app holds keeps what it has.
- **Kept on this Mac** in `keys.json` in Companion's folder (only what differs from the usual keys, so a job never changed keeps Companion's own). Delete everything removes it. It is checked when read: a file that is there but cannot be read as text, an entry not on its job's list, or two jobs on the same keys, go back to the usual keys, the person is told once at launch, and the repaired file is written back. The hold to talk choice in `talk-keys.json` is read when there is no `keys.json` yet, and that old file is removed once `keys.json` is written.
- **At launch** each job's saved keys are tried, then its usual ones, and nothing else. Use a suggestion's keys are only tried and let go again, since T11 takes them from other apps only while a Prompt help result shows; each result tries them again the same way (the keys in use, else the saved then the usual ones), so keys another app held at launch work from the first result after it lets go. One notice says what another app holds: Stop everything first and plainly (its key does not work right now, and Stop everything is still in the menu bar icon), then the jobs without keys, hold to talk, and each job on its usual keys for now.
- **Every line that names a key reads the keys in use**: the hints at the end of the first run, Settings, the practice, the Prompt help result (which names its use keys only when they could be taken for that result, since another app may have taken them after launch, and names the keys taken, which can differ from the ones Settings showed), the error explainer's words. Lessons are written with the usual keys and shown and said with the keys in use at that moment; a job without keys is named by its job, with where to pick others, never by keys that do not work. INSTALL.md names the usual keys and says each can be changed.
- **Settings, Keys to remember** (Home's Settings tab): each job, its keys, a Change button with the short list (only keys the free check finds free can be pressed, so use a suggestion's keys, which are tried there because they are registered only for a result, show as taken when another app took them since; hold to talk's say what they give up), and Reset to the usual keys. Use a suggestion's row says its keys work only while a Prompt help suggestion shows. After a change one line says what the job uses now, and the keyboard goes back to that row's Change button; keys another app took since the list was asked for make the list be asked for again, so they show as taken.

## Settled in the build (19 Oct 2026, HeyClicky v1.0.53 parity, the shell and teaching side)
Build details, not new founder decisions. Each new behaviour a person might want different is a setting that is off, or as before, until turned on; the founder picks the defaults.
- **The island says what is next.** Where it would say Ready, after anything that needs the person: "Not connected yet" while no Claude key or plan is connected, then "Version X is ready" once a check found an update (the shell tells every window, `update-ready`). Neither shows the mark. Home shows the update as a notice on every tab with Install, pressed by the person, never while something runs (the page asks `updates.ts installAllowed`; the shell refuses too while the engine says a task or an approval is under way, `updates.rs Running`).
- **The island and Home open with a short motion.** The island's downward grow (0.18 s) and Home's drop when it opens (from 6 points higher, fading in), instant with Reduce motion or Less motion; a resize of Home is never animated, and the part of the island in the menu bar never moves (`island.rs`).
- **Home steps aside for what it opens** (`home.stepAside`, off unless turned on): after a link, the email for Report a problem, or Finder showing the support log, Home closes the way Esc does, unless something on it waits for the person. Not built for the folder chooser: it is another process's dialog and most likely sits above Home already; checked in the session below.
- **Follow up by pointing** (`talk.followUpByPointing`, off unless turned on): holding the talk keys while the pointer is on a finished task's result in Home puts the words into its follow up box. Only the pointer entering and leaving that part of Home's own window is noticed. Where the words go is decided as the keys go down (with Home showing), never when the words come back, and the box must still show then; the shell tells the page whenever Home goes away, so a pointer left resting on the result never counts.

## Open, to settle on a real Mac
How the island looks and behaves around the notch, in full screen apps, and on a second display; the hands on session checks it.

Item 1: not proven until a real Mac session (rule 18; nothing here was launched): that the island lines up with the notch on each MacBook model and on a second display; that the tracking area reports the pointer while another app is in front; that a click on the island reaches it while another app is in front; that full screen apps hide it; that the island at window level 27 never covers a pop up menu; that the pill in the middle of the menu bar does not cover the menu titles of an app with many menus (Xcode, Office) on a narrow display without a notch, such as a 1440 point MacBook Air; that the lip under the notch and the status lines, which hang just below the menu bar while shown, do not get in the way of the front app's title or toolbar; how the thin line behaves with a menu bar that hides itself; that 0.4 seconds to open and 3 seconds to follow to another display feel right; that the click elsewhere watch and the display follow need no permission, and the watch closes Home only when it should; how Home and the island feel together.

For item 2: how the trip feels at 60 steps a second on a real panel (and on a second display); whether the clear zone and Home's column (checked against the island's own sizes in the tests) feel right around the real notch and Home; whether staying home for a window that fills the screen is right (most people keep their browser that way, so the character would rarely come); whether the arrow leaving from the character as it sets off reads well, or whether the founder would rather the character arrive first and then point (that would delay the ring, its words and its watch by up to a second); and that Home, opening or growing under the notch, never ends over the character beside a window.

Every key (wave 4): that a key changed in Settings works at once in another app and the old one stops; that use a suggestion's keys are free for other apps while no result shows, and that keys another app held at launch work for a result once that app lets go; that Change greys out keys another app holds (the same limit as hold to talk: macOS refuses only keys taken alone); that a damaged `keys.json` gives one notice and then none; that VoiceOver users can reach every Change button. Also not settled: the letter keys are named by their place on a US keyboard (the hot key service works by place), so on an AZERTY keyboard the usual A (use a suggestion) is the key labelled Q, and on Dvorak every letter is somewhere else; and with VoiceOver on, Control and Option is VoiceOver's own key, so Control, Option and Command with a letter may be one of VoiceOver's commands. Both are reasons someone would change keys, and both need a person on a real Mac.

HeyClicky v1.0.53's fixed failure cases, added to the founder's hands on session (19 Oct 2026; none was tried): Mission Control and Spaces thumbnails with the island, the character and a ring up, with Hide from screen sharing on (all panels are Stationary and CanJoinAllSpaces, and the sharing setting changes how windows are shared); sleep and wake, and plugging in a display, with Home open, and with Settings open (no beachball, everything still placed right); AirPods connecting, and switching off, while the talk keys are held; the folder chooser (Change folder, Open from a link) and Report a problem with Home open (is either covered by Home; with Step aside on, does Home close after the email or Finder opens and come back with its line when opened again); the lid closed with an external display (hold to talk listens with another microphone, or says the lid line with none; the built in display choice moves to the main display); starting a second copy from Finder (it leaves quietly, `instance.rs`). Also from this build: that the pointer entering Home's result is noticed while another app is in front (follow up by pointing), and that moving off it while the words are turned into text still fills its follow up box; that the island's grow and Home's drop redraw cleanly in the web view and feel short; that the pointer leaving the island within the 0.18 s grow leaves it at its rest size (not hanging below the menu bar), and that Home resizing during its drop ends at the new height (`set_frame_moving`, `island::Moves`); that waiting for a slow page (up to 3 s) feels right in a real browser, and that Stop everything during the wait takes no picture; the lid closed with a wired headset in the headphone jack (hold to talk listens with it, T13).

From the audit fixes (20 Oct 2026; built and tested with fakes, not tried): opening Companion again from Finder or Spotlight while it runs shows it if Hide hid it, else opens Home without the keyboard (`RunEvent::Reopen`); Hide, and a full screen app, take every ring away; a window maximized on a display that keeps nothing free (no menu bar and no Dock there: a second display with Displays have separate Spaces off, or a menu bar that hides itself) no longer hides Companion, because on such a display a window that fills it counts as full screen only when the desktop picture's windows are gone too. Their layers were measured on this Mac on an ordinary Space (WindowManager one level either side of the desktop level); that they are gone on a full screen Space, and that a full screen video on such a display still hides Companion, needs the hands on session (`fullscreen.rs`).

Hold to talk: whether macOS refuses Option and Space when another app (ChatGPT, Raycast, Alfred, Superwhisper) holds it, or lets both have it (a launch blocker, see T13); whether letting go of Option before Space still ends the hold; and whether the press repeats while held (T13 open items).
