# Can Squibby be B2B? (fact checked)

Research date: 13 Nov 2026. Fact check pass on the same day: every source below was reopened and compared with the claim. Anything that could not be confirmed is marked **unconfirmed**. Most surveys here are run or paid for by vendors who sell into this market, so treat their percentages as directional.

## What the fact check changed

- **ProjectDiscovery:** the "trust requirements" (audit trails, scoped credentials, human review) were not survey results. They are properties of ProjectDiscovery's own product, Neo. Removed as buyer evidence. The survey was commissioned by ProjectDiscovery and covers North America and Western Europe.
- **Gartner quote:** fixed. The real wording is "continuous monitoring, enforced guardrails, rapid rollback mechanisms" plus "circuit breakers that halt agent operation on threshold violations", and it applies only to the highest autonomy level. "Clear ownership" was not in the article. The 40% prediction has no target date in the article.
- **PocketOS:** added that Railway later helped recover the data. The page presents the Cursor and Opus 4.6 attribution as allegations.
- **GitGuardian 3.2% vs 1.5%:** now **confirmed** on GitGuardian's own blog (was marked unconfirmed). Added the 34% year on year rise and the 1,275,105 AI service secrets.
- **Turbot:** it does not use the exact phrase "EDR for AI coding agents". Its heading is "AI EDR: an inspection layer for coding agents". Its hooks do not yet cover every agent it discovers. The buyer is implied (security teams), not stated.
- **Vendr / Retool:** Vendr's page calls $66,000 both the median (159 purchases) and the "Avg Contract Value" (110 deals). Treat it as a rough midpoint, not a clean median.
- **Cursor:** added that Cursor Teams already has admin enforced **Team Rules** across the whole organization. That weakens "shared house rules" as a unique pitch (see section 2).
- **Added:** Claude Team seat prices, YC's current Requests for Startups (nothing matching), and a list of questions a YC partner would ask.

## Verdict in one paragraph

Yes, but not as an enterprise security product, at least not yet. Shape (a), a guardrail and audit layer on every employee laptop, is a real buyer need. It is also already a crowded, funded category that sells to security teams (CISOs and AppSec): Palo Alto Networks (Koi), Endor Labs, Cycode, GitGuardian, Turbot and others. Anthropic and Cursor now ship central admin controls themselves. A solo founder cannot win a CISO deal against them in 2026: no SOC 2, no admin console, no MDM rollout. Shape (b), non engineers building internal tools, is also served by funded cloud platforms (Lovable, Superblocks, Retool, and YC's Runtime). The gap Squibby may fit is **small teams with no security team**: 5 to 50 person startups and agencies where engineers and non engineers run several coding agents on their own Macs. **The smallest B2B offer is "Squibby for Teams":** shared hard blocks (enforced by hooks, not prompts) across all seven tools, one team feed of plain words receipts, the existing Undo and saved copies, and the secrets guard. Sell it founder led as a paid 30 day pilot to a handful of small teams. This is a hypothesis: nothing below shows small teams will pay for it. That is the first thing to test.

## 1. Buyer evidence: is the pain real?

**Incidents**
- PocketOS, 24 Apr 2026: a Cursor agent, reportedly running Claude Opus 4.6, was on a staging task and used a broadly scoped Railway API token to delete a Railway volume. That removed the production database and its volume level backups. Railway later helped recover the data and patch safeguards. The page presents the Cursor and model attribution as allegations. Source: [AI Incident Database, incident 1469](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1469).
- Squibby's "saved copy before any agent's destructive command" maps onto this kind of incident only partly. A saved copy of local files would not have saved a cloud database deleted through an API. Only blocking the call (or flagging a broadly scoped token) would.

**Surveys (all vendor run or vendor sponsored)**
- ProjectDiscovery, 28 Apr 2026: 200 security practitioners in North America and Western Europe, at organizations with more than 500 staff, surveyed Feb to Mar 2026, commissioned by ProjectDiscovery. 78% ranked secrets exposure as the top challenge AI assisted coding introduced or amplified. Only 38% said their team is comfortably keeping pace with code volume. Source: [ProjectDiscovery](https://projectdiscovery.io/blog/the-trust-gap-behind-the-ai-coding-boom-what-200-security-practitioners-just-told-us).
- Purple Book Community and ArmorCode, reported 23 Mar 2026: more than 650 senior security leaders in North America and Europe. 59% confirmed or suspected shadow AI, 73% said AI assisted development is outpacing what security can review, and 70% confirmed or suspected vulnerabilities from AI generated code in production. Source: [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/03/purple-book-community-and-armorcode-survey-flags-shadow-ai-ai-generated-code-risks).
- GitGuardian State of Secrets Sprawl 2026 (17 Mar 2026): about 28.65 million new hardcoded secrets reached public GitHub in 2025, up 34% year on year. AI service secrets reached 1,275,105, up 81%. Commits assisted by Claude Code had a 3.2% secret leak rate against a 1.5% baseline across all public commits. Sources: [GitGuardian blog](https://blog.gitguardian.com/the-state-of-secrets-sprawl-2026/), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/15/product-showcase-gitguardian-ggshield-ai-hook/). Caveat: GitGuardian sells the fix, and the 3.2% figure is a correlation in public repos, not proof the agent caused the leaks.

**Analysts**
- Gartner, reported 27 May 2026: 40% of organizations are set to demote or decommission AI agents because they struggle to govern them (no target date given in the article). For the highest autonomy level, Gartner's Shiva Varma said governance needs "continuous monitoring, enforced guardrails, rapid rollback mechanisms" and circuit breakers that halt agents on threshold violations. Source: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/AI-ML/2026/05/27/4-in-10-ai-agents-headed-for-demotion-or-the-rubbish-bin/5246964). "Rapid rollback" is a fair way to describe Squibby's Undo. (The Gartner press release itself was not opened.)
- Gartner's figures on "guardian agents" budget share (under 1% today, 5 to 7% by 2028) are not in The Register article. **Unconfirmed.**

**Market signal from M&A**
- Palo Alto Networks announced it would acquire Koi on 17 Feb 2026, to secure agentic endpoints. Its chief product and technology officer, Lee Klarich, said "AI agents and tools are the ultimate insiders." Terms were not disclosed; CyberScoop cites Israeli outlet Globes for a price of about $400 million (**unconfirmed** by Palo Alto). Source: [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/palo-alto-networks-acquires-koi-agentic-ai-security/).

**Read:** the pain is real and named by security buyers at large companies: secrets leaking, review that cannot keep up, no audit trail, no rollback. Big security companies are paying for it. None of these sources measure the pain at 5 to 50 person teams, which is where Squibby would sell. That gap is the main open question.

**The platforms themselves are closing the gap:**
- **Claude Code** (official docs): Claude for Teams and Enterprise Owners can push server managed settings from claude.ai, including permission deny lists, `allowManagedPermissionRulesOnly`, and organization wide hooks such as an audit script after every Edit or Write. Organizations with MDM can deploy endpoint managed settings instead. Audit log events for settings changes are available through the compliance API or audit log export. Source: [code.claude.com server managed settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/server-managed-settings).
- **Cursor** (pricing page): Teams is "$30+" per user per month. Enterprise adds "auto-run, browser, and network controls", repository, model and MCP access controls, and audit logs. Source: [cursor.com/pricing](https://cursor.com/pricing).
- **Cursor Team Rules:** Teams and Enterprise admins can create rules in the dashboard and make them required for every member. Source: [Cursor rules docs](https://cursor.com/docs/context/rules). These are instructions to the model, not hard blocks, which per AGENTS.md rule 12 is not where safety should live. Squibby's edge is enforcement through hooks, not "shared rules" as such.

**Read:** single tool controls are now built in, including org wide rules in Cursor and org wide deny lists and hooks in Claude Code. What none of the platforms gives you is **one** policy and **one** plain words record across all seven tools, plus Undo that does not touch git. The security vendors do cover several tools (Turbot six, GitGuardian five), but they sell to security teams and offer no undo and no layer that explains changes to a non engineer. Caveat: Turbot already covers many of the same agents through hooks, so "multi tool" alone is not a moat.

That is the difference worth selling. It also means Squibby cannot offer what IT asks for first (SSO, a central console, approved data connections) without building a server.

## 4. Who buys, and how deals happen

- **Shape (a), enterprise:** security teams (CISO, AppSec, incident response). The vendors in section 2 write for them and offer MDM deployment and central dashboards ([Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/ai-guardrails-real-time-ide-security/), [GitGuardian](https://www.gitguardian.com/developer-endpoint-protection), [Turbot](https://turbot.com/blog/2026/06/introducing-guardian)). Sales are demo led with no public prices.
- **Shape (b), enterprise:** IT sets the guardrails and business teams build ([Superblocks](https://www.superblocks.com/blog/ai-app-flood-how-enterprises-are-governing-it)), or engineering controls the sandbox ([Runtime](https://www.runtm.com/blog/runtime-yc-p26/)). The benchmark is Retool at roughly $66k a year ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/retool)).
- **Small teams:** the buyer is the founder or eng lead, who already buys seats like Cursor Teams ($30+ per user a month, [Cursor](https://cursor.com/pricing)), Claude Team ($20 standard or $100 premium per seat a month on annual billing, $25 or $125 monthly; Team includes Claude Code, [Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)) or Retool Team ($10 per builder a month annual, [Retool](https://retool.com/pricing)). This is a bottom up, card on file motion a solo founder can run.
- **Early sales advice (YC, 2016, old but still useful):** a YC Startup School Radio episode with Brian Trautschold suggests about a hundred discovery conversations, asking whether people would pay for a feature set before building it, and bending the product only when deal size justifies it (the test: will it help the next 20 customers?) ([YC blog](https://ycombinator.com/blog/balancing-b2b-sales)). Advice attributed to newer Startup School talks (few paying design partners, success metrics set before a pilot, SOC 2 coming up early) came from third party summaries: **unconfirmed**.
- **YC's current Requests for Startups (Winter 2027)** has 11 items; none is about agent safety, non engineers building software, or small business AI tools ([YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)). Squibby would not be riding a stated YC request, which is fine, but the pitch has to stand on traction.

## 5. What a solo founder could sell first

**Do not start with:** CISO or enterprise deals. They need SOC 2, an admin console, MDM packaging and SSO, and the competition is Palo Alto, Endor, Cycode, GitGuardian and Turbot.

**Start with "Squibby for Teams" (5 to 50 seats, startups and agencies):**
1. **Shared hard blocks:** one team file of rules (block `rm -rf`, block reads of `.env`, block force push, block deploys and broadly scoped cloud tokens without a tap) that every teammate's Squibby enforces through hooks across all seven tools. Squibby already has the per user version. Pitch the difference from Cursor Team Rules: these are enforced, not suggested.
2. **Team receipts feed:** the existing plain words review and receipts, also posted to a shared place the team picks (Slack or a shared folder): "Priya's Cursor deleted 3 files in billing/, Undo available." An eng lead can read it without opening a diff.
3. **Undo and saved copies:** already built. Gartner's own words for the need are "rapid rollback mechanisms." Be honest that it covers local files, not cloud resources (see PocketOS).
4. **Keys never pushed:** the existing secrets guard, with a weekly team report. GitGuardian's 3.2% vs 1.5% figure is the hook.
5. **Non engineer mode:** "Hand Squibby the job" plus proof (project checks and a browser check) lets an ops or marketing person build an internal tool, and the eng lead sees a proven receipt before anything goes live. That is shape (b) delivered from the laptop, with no cloud platform to adopt.

**Price to test (a hypothesis, not evidence):** about $20 to $30 per seat per month on top of Pro. For reference, that equals a Claude Team standard seat ($20 to $25) and sits near Cursor Teams ($30+). A guard layer costing as much as the agent itself may be a hard sell; test a lower team add on as well. Or a flat paid 30 day pilot for 3 to 5 teams, with success metrics agreed up front: destructive commands caught, undos used, secrets blocked.

**Who to call first:** small agencies and startups where non engineers already use Cursor or Claude Code. Incidents like [PocketOS](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1469) make the cold opener easy.

## 6. Questions a YC partner would ask (not yet answered)

1. **Who has paid?** How many small teams have you talked to, and did any of them pay or sign a pilot? The evidence above is all about large companies.
2. **Why won't Anthropic and Cursor do this?** Both already ship org wide controls. Answer needed: cross tool enforcement, plain words review, Undo, and non engineers, and why each platform has little reason to cover its rivals.
3. **Why not Turbot or GitGuardian moving down market?** They already hook several agents. What stops them adding a cheap team plan?
4. **How does it reach every Mac with no MDM?** Small teams have no IT. What is the install story, and what happens when one teammate turns Squibby off?
5. **Where does the team data live?** A team feed is a new data flow (see section 6). Is it on device plus a shared webhook, or a server you run and secure?
6. **What is the measurable outcome?** Incidents blocked per seat per month, or hours saved on review. Pick one before the first pilot.
7. **Market size:** how many 5 to 50 person teams run several coding agents? No source above counts them: **unknown**.
8. **Mac only:** many teams mix Mac and Windows. How much of a team can you cover today?

## 7. Conflicts with Squibby's own rules (flag for the founder)

- **Data leaving the device.** A team feed and a central policy need a server, and receipts would leave the laptop. AGENTS.md keeps memory and captures on the device. A team feed is a new kind of data flow and needs a founder decision. Option: keep it on device and let the team share a folder or Slack webhook the user sets up, so there is no Squibby server.
- **Safety in hooks, not prompts.** Team rules must ship as hook enforced blocks (AGENTS.md rule 12), which is also the selling point against Cursor Team Rules.
- **Claude usage.** Companies on Claude Team or Enterprise bring their own Claude. That fits rule 1 (never route usage). The subscription route stays behind its remote switch.
- **Overlap with Anthropic.** Claude Code's managed settings already push deny rules and hooks for Team and Enterprise ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/server-managed-settings)). Squibby must sell what Anthropic does not do: seven tools under one policy, plain words, Undo, and a layer for non engineers.
