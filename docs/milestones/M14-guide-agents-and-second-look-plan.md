## Part 2: Second Look, the helper HeyClicky does not have

**The problem everyone with an LLM has:** the answer sounds sure and may be wrong, and you cannot tell. CEOs, founders, engineers and beginners all get burned the same way: a wrong number in a plan, a citation that does not exist, code that quietly misbehaves.

**What it does:** you select an AI answer in any app (or press the key with it on screen) and press a key. The light comes on first (rule 5). Within about ten seconds a card says one of three honest things:
- **Solid:** what it checked and how.
- **Check these:** the specific claims that look shaky, why, and what to verify, with a source when it found one.
- **I can't tell:** what it could not check. It never fakes certainty.

For numbers it redoes the arithmetic itself. For facts it searches and shows sources. For code it flags the lines that look risky and says what test would prove them. For quotes and citations it says which it could not find. Companion's character reacts (a nod, a squint), which is the fun: a small "caught one" moment, never a score or a streak (rule 16).

**Why us:** it is the same idea as the hero ("done means proven") turned on any AI answer, and the engine for it already exists: the second model review (it caught 5 of 5 risky changes with no false alarms on real Claude), the text read on a hotkey (T11), the web search lane with sources (Research), and the redaction rules.

**What it is not:** it does not decide what is true. It shows its work, and says so when it cannot tell.

**Rules it follows:** the selected text is read only on the person's key press with the light on (5, 8); nothing is stored; secrets are hidden before anything is sent (redact); a check costs pennies and shows its estimate (T10).

**How we know it works (before anyone sees it):** an eval of 100 AI answers, 50 with a known seeded mistake (a wrong number, an invented citation, an out of date fact, a subtle logic slip, a wrong code detail) and 50 correct. Measures: how many mistakes it catches, how often it cries wolf, and how often it says "I can't tell" when it should. Targets: catch at least 85%, false alarms at most 10%, and never confidently wrong. The real run needs credits; the corpus, the prompts, the engine and the scorer are built first, with stand ins, like the other eval sets. Real run cost estimate: about $1 to $2.

**The fun, later:** "Spot what the AI got wrong": the seeded answers from the eval turned into a practice game that teaches people to catch mistakes. It is also a shareable result card. Built after Second Look works.

### What happens after the person answers (22 Oct 2026, a found bug, fixed)

**What the person sees and what each answer sends (before the fix).** After a vague request the plan card says "Stopped after 0 of 3 steps: a step needs your answer". Under it the receipt of step 1 shows Claude's words ending on the question ("It needs your answer") and the follow up box, with the line "Type your answer below. It carries on from here, with a new undo point and a new check." There is no Try again and no Carry on for this case (Try again is hidden for a question; Carry on, `drive.resume`, is only for a plan paused because the person changed files). Pressing Send in the follow up box sends one `agent.send` with the answer text. The engine (`AgentRunner.followUp`) saw the last task had asked, and ran **one** new task from the answer ("The person answered your question ... Carry on with the task"). It was a single task, not a step of the plan: its own undo point, check and receipt, but `inPlan` unset. The plan itself was never kept, so **steps 2 and 3 never ran and nothing said so**. Proved by `runner/test/plan-answer.test.ts`, which failed 9 of 12 on the old code.

**The fix, in the smallest correct way.** When a step of a plan stops on a question (`needs_you`), the engine keeps the rest of the plan with that task (`LastTask.plan`: the steps, the step that asked, the model, the goal and the money the plan had left). The answer (`agent.send`, no new command and no protocol change) now runs that step again with the answer, as a step of the plan (`Step k of n`, its own undo point, check and receipt), then runs the steps that were waiting, in the same Claude conversation (off the record, each later step is told the goal plus the answer). One `drive.done` ends the carried on run. Nothing else changed: the same safety hooks, the same approval cards (a command that touches git still waits for a tap), the plan keeps only the money it had left, and the daily limit applies. The plan is forgotten, as the task always was, when the person starts any other task, undoes the step that asked, or the engine restarts. A second question stops the plan again and the next answer carries it on from there. The answer is never a task's name: the task list shows "Step 1 of 3: ..." and a pasted key is hidden before Claude sees it.

**The words.** The card under the question now reads: "Type your answer below. This step finishes with it, then the rest of the plan carries on, each step with its own undo point and check." The plan card reads "Stopped after 0 of 3 steps: a step needs your answer, and the plan carries on when you give it." (`app/src/strings.ts`; the receipt now carries `inPlan` so the page can tell a plan's question from a single task's.)
