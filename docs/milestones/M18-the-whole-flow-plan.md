## What research said (short)
- **Finch** (a self care pet app) starts by hatching an egg, then naming the pet and picking its traits, so it feels like your own from the first minute. One button stays the same size, colour and place on every screen. A design critique faulted it for showing progress only late, so people did not know how much was left.
- Most people who drop off do it in the first session (Userpilot), so the first minute has to show the point of the app, not a form.
- Personalisation first (Airtable asks your role before anything else) makes the rest feel made for you.
- **Raycast's** Settings: a left side list with General first, one tab per feature, and one place for every shortcut.
- **Apple:** Settings opens with Command comma; a menu bar app keeps its windows simple and uses the Mac's own controls.

## The first run (in Home, under the notch)
The water at the bottom of the panel rises with every step: it is the progress bar, and "1 of 5" says it in words too. The lime button is always in the same place.
1. **Hello.** A drop falls into the water and Flowflow rises out of it. "Your AI built it. Let's finish it." One line on what it does. Then the recorded fix (today's first look), before anything is asked.
2. **Who do you want with you?** Flowflow, chosen, beside empty places for the companions to come (see decision 2).
3. **Make it yours.** Its name (Flowflow unless changed), how lively it is (Lively or Calm, the reactions and motion settings), and where it lives (left, right, a corner, or only in the notch).
4. **What should it help with?** The four groups as plain switches (Fix my project, Show me how, Talk to it, Drop files on it), each saying what it will ask for, and that it asks only the first time, never at install.
5. **Connect your Claude.** The key goes into the Keychain (today's flow, restyled). A plan login stays in Anthropic's own flow, unchanged.
6. **Which project are we finishing?** Drop a folder or pick a recent one.
7. **Ready.** The panel is full of water. The real shortcuts from the app (not made up), then "Watch it fix a practice project" (today's recorded first look) or "Skip, take me to my project".

## Settings
A Mac style window with a grouped side list and the Mac's own controls:
- **Companion:** a live Flowflow on the water at the top that you can switch between idle, working and done, then its name, the companion, how lively, where it lives, its size and hiding at the edge. Then Look and motion, Talking and listening.
- **Powers:** one card per group with its switch, its tools as small chips, and what it asks for.
- **Your Mac:** what it may do, keys, screen sharing. **Account:** your Claude key, spending, updates.
- Every existing setting keeps its place in the registry (T16) and keeps search, Reset and Undo; only the grouping and the look change.

## How it is built
Tests first for the step order, the water level for each step, and that choosing in the first run writes the same settings Settings shows (name, liveliness, edge, groups). The existing checks stay: the key never reaches a page, permissions are asked on first use only, nothing is stored about the screen. The full suite and the app build after each part, nothing merged without the founder.
