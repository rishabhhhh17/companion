# What Companion reads, when, and where it goes (friends alpha, build 0.1.0)

**Draft for the founder to review before it is sent to anyone.** Written from what the code does today. It makes no claim about how Anthropic handles data: read their terms for the key you use.

## The short version
- Companion looks at your screen or listens **only when you press its key**, with a light on first. It never watches in the background.
- It uses **your own Claude key**. Companion has no server and never sees your key, your project or your screen: what Claude is sent goes from your Mac to Anthropic, under your account.
- Other sites hear from your Mac only where the table below says: your own Vercel tool, a project you open from a link, packages a check installs, web pages a task opens, and downloads you press for (the speech model, the browser check, updates).
- Nothing you did is kept anywhere but on your Mac, and **Settings, Delete everything** removes what Companion keeps in its own folders. The Mac's own temporary folder is not Companion's: while a task runs, Claude Code can leave the output of a command there, and macOS clears it by itself.

## Feature by feature
| When | What Companion reads | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| You press **F** (point at the problem) | Runs your project's own checks and reads the failing line from your files. Then looks at the screen you are on, **on your Mac**, to find that line (the Mac's own text reader). If the line is not there, it looks at your other screens one after another, with the light still on. The pictures are never saved and never sent. When your project's own checks pass (or there is nothing to check) and the folder is linked to Vercel, your own Vercel tool also asks Vercel for your last deploy and, if it failed, its build log, to point at the line that stopped it. | Nothing of your project or your screen leaves your Mac for this step. The Vercel tool asks Vercel under your own Vercel login, only reading, never a deploy. Turn that off in Settings with Read my last Vercel deploy log when looking for a problem. This row is for a project with a code tool or a browser in front; for anything else, see the next row (You press **F** with no project). |
| You press **F** with no project, or with another kind of app in front (a spreadsheet, a document, Mail, System Settings) | Companion's own code learns only the kind of app in front (a code tool, a browser or another app), never its name. Then one picture of the display you are on, like **S**, light on first. The picture shows whatever is on that display, the app in front and its name included. Card numbers, bank numbers, keys and tokens the text reader finds are blacked out, and your password manager's windows and Companion's own are left out. The words the Mac's text reader found on the screen are also checked on your Mac for a spreadsheet error such as #REF!, which Companion explains from its own list. Each press of Check it is fixed, each step of the fix and each answer to Did that work? takes one more picture the same way. | The picture and the words the Mac read on it (up to 60 lines for each display, the blacked out ones left out) are sent to Claude under your key, to name the problem and the steps. A look again also sends the name of the spreadsheet error the first look read, such as #REF!, so Companion can tell whether it is gone. Nothing of it is kept (in memory only, like **S**). Turn this off in Settings with The stuck key works in any app; then **F** only checks your project. |
| You press **S** (ask about your screen) | One picture of the display you are on. Card numbers, bank numbers, keys and tokens that the text reader finds are blacked out first. Your password manager's windows and Companion's own are left out. Which app is in front (its id only, nothing in it), so that with Teach everyday apps in their own words on in Settings the answer can be a tutor for that app; Claude is told only its common name, such as Mail, and only when that setting is on. | The picture (in memory only, never written to disk by Companion) is sent to Claude with your question, under your key. Claude Code itself keeps a copy of any picture it receives in its temporary folder; Companion points that folder at a drive in memory and erases it after each answer. |
| You press **R** (Prompt help, in another app) | The text in the box you are typing in, in the app in front, read through macOS Accessibility (macOS asks the first time). Each app is asked about first (Always, Once or Never); a password box, secure typing, password managers, Keychain Access, Passwords and System Settings are never read. Code, links, commands and error text in it are set aside on your Mac and put back afterwards. With it, unless Use project info in Prompt help is off in Settings: your chosen project's name, its script and package names, its top level file and folder names and its house rules (anything that looks like a key hidden first); and up to 300 words of what Companion remembers (How much Claude is told from memory sets the amount, and none with Tell Claude what Companion remembers off). | Sent to Claude under your key. The improved text goes back into the box only when you use it. Nothing of it is kept. |
| You press **E** (explain an error) | The text you selected in any app, read through macOS Accessibility (macOS asks the first time), with the same per app answer as **R** and the same apps never read. Colours are removed and anything that looks like a key is hidden first. When it names a line in your project, that line is found on your Mac and goes with it. | Sent to Claude under your key, which answers in two plain sentences. Nothing of it is kept. |
| You hold Option and Space (talk, or the other keys you picked in Settings) | Your voice, only while you hold the keys, from the microphone picked in Settings (or your Mac's default). So your project's own names come out right, a short list of names from the project folder you chose: package names from `package.json`, the setting names a project check found, and file and folder names. Never what is inside your files, never a value, never a `.env` file, never a folder whose name sounds private, and never anything that looks like a key. | Turned into words on your Mac by a speech model; the list of names goes only to that model, on your Mac. The audio and the list are never saved or sent; the words go in the box and are sent only if you send them. |
| You ask for a task, a fix or a plan | The files of the project you chose, as Claude needs them. | Sent to Claude under your key, like any use of Claude Code. Companion's safety rules limit what it may change, and every change is snapshotted first so you can undo it. Rescue also asks Vercel for your last deploy's log through your own Vercel tool, the same way and under the same setting as **F**. |
| A task finishes (Explain after a task, on unless turned off in Settings) | The lines the task changed (never settings or key files, installed packages or build output), with anything that looks like a key hidden. Not for a task off the record. | Sent to Claude under your key for two short reasons and a check question, shown under the result. |
| You press **Draw it** | Your question, what the failing check said, a few lines of code around the failing line (anything that looks like a key hidden), your project's package names, some folder names and what its pages serve and ask for. | Sent to Claude under your key; the sketch is drawn on your screen and never saved. |
| A task or Research uses the web | Research always searches the web. A task may too, while Let tasks search the web is on (usual), and may open a web page it needs, such as a library's help page. | The search goes through Claude under your key. A page a task opens is fetched by Claude Code from your Mac, so that site sees your internet address like any visit. |
| You open a project **from a link** | The link you typed (a public GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket project). | Your Mac copies the project from that site with git, so the site sees your internet address. Nothing is sent to Claude for this. |
| A check finds the project's packages missing or changed | The project's own package list (`package.json` and its lock file). Only the checks after a task do this: **F** never installs anything, and Companion never installs Python packages. | The check installs them first, from the registry the project uses (npm, say), the way a terminal would, so the registry sees your internet address and which packages are asked for. Install packages for a check in Settings can ask you first or never install. |
| Claude asks you one short question under a result, and you type your answer | Your answer. Claude is told never to ask for a key or a password (it says which setting to add and where, so you add it yourself), and anything in your answer that looks like one is hidden first. | Sent to Claude, under your key, as the next step of that task. The task is named in What I did by Claude's question, never by your answer. |
| You drag a file onto Companion or paste one into the ask box, then press **Start** | That file only. Text and code files are read on your Mac, and anything that looks like a key or a password is hidden first. Settings and key files (such as `.env` or `id_rsa`) are never read. | Sent to Claude with your request, under your key, only when you press Start. A picture is first looked at by Claude, like **S** (Claude Code's copy goes to a drive in memory and is erased afterwards), and only what it shows, in words, goes with the task. Nothing of the file is kept by Companion. |
| You start a **lesson** (Lessons button) | For each step except in the setting practice's lesson, one picture of your screen like **S** (light on first, same covering of private text), to find the place to point at. The project's own files are checked to prove a step worked (a setting's name, whether a file exists; setting values and key files are never read). Where you are in a lesson is saved as step names and outcomes only. | The picture goes to Claude under your key, like **S**. The saved place stays on your Mac. |
| You start a **practice project** (the first run, or the Learn tab) | Nothing of yours. Companion copies a small project that ships inside the app into a new folder in your home folder (named after the practice, never over anything) and adds one house rule to it so its check cannot be changed. Find runs that copy's own checks; Fix it with me works on that copy only. | The copy is a folder in your home folder like any project. Fix it with me sends that copy's files to Claude under your key (about 20 cents each, an estimate), like any task; the missing setting practice uses no Claude at all: its lesson tells you each step in words and never looks at your screen. Which practices you started, found and finished, and where their copies are, is kept in Companion's own folder. Remove my copy and Start over move a copy to the Trash, only after you confirm. |
| You hold the talk keys for the first time | Nothing from your Mac. It is a plain download. | Companion asks first, then downloads its speech model (about 140 MB) from Hugging Face (`huggingface.co`) only when you press Download, so that site sees your internet address like any website. The file is checked against its known fingerprint and kept in Companion's own folder; Delete everything removes it. |
| Once a day, and when you press **Check for updates** (once updates are set up) | Nothing from your Mac. It is a plain request for one file. | One web address (the founder's release page) is asked for the newest version, so it sees your internet address like any website. Updates are installed only when you press Install, and only if they match Companion's own key. You can turn the daily check off in Settings. |
| You press **Get the browser check** (on a check result, once) | Nothing from your Mac. It is a plain download. | One download (about 100 MB) from Playwright's servers (`cdn.playwright.dev`; Playwright is made by Microsoft), so they see your internet address like any website. It is the hidden browser Companion opens your pages in, plus a small video tool (about 1 MB) that Playwright's installer always adds and Companion does not use. Nothing downloads until you press the button, and Stop everything ends it. It is kept in Companion's own folder; Delete everything removes it. |
| You turn on **Speak to me** or **Answer out loud when I ask by voice** (Settings; both off by default) | Short lines Companion itself wrote (a lesson step, what a ring points at), or the short answer to a question you asked by holding the talk keys. Never anything that looks like a key, card number or token. Whether your Mac's sound is on (read only), so nothing is said while it is muted. | Said out loud by the Mac voice picked in Settings, at the speed picked there. Nothing is sent anywhere. |
| You add a **watch** for a web address, and Companion is open (Settings, **Watch routines**, on unless turned off) | The web address you typed, only that one: a plain request on the schedule you chose (every 5 minutes at the most often), with no cookies, no password, at most 3 redirects, and never to a private or link local address unless you typed localhost for your own dev server. Only the answer's number is kept, never the page. An address with a part after a ? is refused, because keys go there. | To that site, so it sees your internet address. Nothing is sent to Claude. Stop everything and quitting Companion stop the checks. |
| You add a watch for your **Vercel deploy** or your **GitHub checks**, or press **Fix it** on a failed one, or run Rescue (Settings, **Read my last failed GitHub run when looking for a problem**) | The latest deploy or run and, for a failure, the end of its log, read through **your own** `vercel` and `gh` tools, only reading. Companion never holds your sign in, and never deploys, re-runs or changes anything there. Anything that looks like a key is hidden first. | To Vercel and GitHub, by your own tools. The evidence goes to Claude only when you press **Fix it** or run Rescue, as part of that task. |
| You add a watch for **your project's own checks** | The project's build and tests, the way Point at the problem runs them, on your files, with no packages installed and without starting the project. They run only after you have run Point at the problem or a task on that project yourself, and they stop the moment you start your own work on it. | Stays on your Mac. Nothing is sent to Claude. |
| You open Home after a break (**Where was I**, Settings, **Show Where was I after a break of**) | Companion's own task history, your watches and what is waiting. A task run off the record is never named. | Stays on your Mac. Claude is not asked. |
| After a fix passes its checks | Opens your page in a hidden browser on your Mac and takes a picture of **your app** (never your screen), before and after. | Stays on your Mac. |
| You go live | Your project's files go to Vercel through **your own Vercel login**, after safety checks and your yes. | To Vercel, by their tool. |
| Always on | The last few things you did, as short notes (what is in `.companion/project.md` in your project, and a small profile in Companion's own folder). You can read, edit and forget every line in **What I know**. | Stays on your Mac. Never health, money, relationships, moods or secrets. |

## What Companion keeps on your Mac
**Beta numbers** are off until you turn them on in Settings. When on, Companion keeps counts per day on your Mac (how often a thing happened; never words, names, files or anything from your screen). It sends nothing. You can read them, copy them to send to the founder, and erase them.

Task history and spending, undo snapshots of your projects, receipt pictures, memory notes, which practice projects you tried and where their copies are (their paths and dates only), the speech model, a hidden browser, settings, the words in the ask box that you have not sent yet (so they are still there when Companion opens again; gone once you send them, never kept off the record, and never when they look like a key or a password) and a small activity log (what happened, never what was typed, said or on screen). **Settings** lists them with sizes. **Delete everything** removes them and your saved key. Your practice copies are folders in your home folder, so Delete everything leaves them unless you tick its line to move them to the Trash too.

## If you send a log
**Settings, Save a log for support** writes a text file with no project content, nothing typed or said and nothing from your screen; your name, folders and keys are covered. You decide whether to send it. **Report a problem** saves the same file, then opens an email in your own mail app with only Companion's version and your Mac's version filled in; you attach the file and send it yourself, or not at all.

## What this note does not say
Companion cannot see what Anthropic or Vercel do with what they receive, and does not claim to. Do not use it on a project you are not allowed to share with Claude.
